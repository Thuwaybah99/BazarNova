01 / THE BUSINESS QUESTION

What really drives
retail performance?

Total sales alone do not tell the whole story. This project examines when demand rises, which product groups contribute value, whether campaigns concentrate sales, and how store mix shapes outcomes. The objective is to connect descriptive analysis to decisions on assortment, campaigns and planning.

01

When is demand strongest?

Examine the trajectory of sales across the year and detect peaks that matter for stock planning.

02

Where does value come from?

Separate volume leadership from revenue contribution across products and hierarchies.

03

Which promotions stand out?

Compare campaign performance and identify concentration in specific product groups.

04

How do stores differ?

Understand the relationship between store results, sales mix and product categories.

04 / FROM OBSERVATION TO ACTION

Recommendations worth testing.

These are proposed next steps grounded in the project's observations, not verified outcomes.

DEMAND PLANNING

Prepare before peak season

Build a pre-December stock and staffing plan; validate seasonal patterns against additional years of data.

PROMOTION STRATEGY

Investigate Promo 14

Identify the offer, product group and timing driving concentration, then test what can transfer to other campaigns.

ASSORTMENT STRATEGY

Balance volume with value

Monitor units, revenue and—when available—margin to prioritize commercially meaningful product mixes.

!

Important analytical limitation

Only 6 of the stated 144 stores were represented in the project analysis. Findings should not be generalized to the full chain without broader coverage. The historical data and descriptive comparisons also do not establish causal campaign impact.

THE TAKEAWAY

Analysis is valuable when
it changes the next question.

This case study demonstrates a practical workflow from structured retail data to business interpretation, while distinguishing observed patterns from hypotheses that require further testing.
