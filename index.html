01 / TIME SERIES

Revenue is concentrated in a small number of recorded periods.

December accounts for about 66.5% of recorded revenue, but available dates cover only the first two days of each month. One promotional event plays a significant role.

03 / STORE COMPARISON

The top stores achieve revenue differently.

Electro World recorded approximately 16,430.01 in revenue on roughly 3,215 units; DIGI recorded approximately 16,061.05 on roughly 4,651 units. This suggests distinct selling mixes.

↗
Business implication

Compare revenue per unit and category distribution before setting store targets or transferring practices.

04 / CAMPAIGN ANALYSIS

The PR16 records deserve a separate investigation.

Eight records assigned to PR16 generated about 13,063.90 in revenue, or 30.7% of the recorded total; all correspond to Portable Heater on December 1.

↗
Business implication

Audit promotion coding, discount mechanics, and transaction validity. A high recorded revenue share does not prove uplift caused by a promotion.

OBSERVATION30.7%

05 / PRODUCT DEMAND

High demand and high revenue are not equivalent.

Product quantity and revenue need to be read together: a product may sell many units but generate less revenue than a premium-priced item. The chart allows investigation of those differences.

↗
Business implication

Classify products by demand and revenue to guide assortment, but obtain costs before assessing profitability.

06 / DATA QUALITY

Stock conclusions depend on record completeness.

Zero-sales and other record-level patterns can highlight observations worth investigating, but they do not by themselves confirm a stockout or overstocking.

↗
Business implication

Validate inventory snapshots, units, dates, and stock movements before using this view for replenishment decisions.

04 / FROM OBSERVATION TO ACTION

Recommendations worth testing.

Proposed actions based on the six Tableau perspectives; they have not been tested as outcomes.

DATA QUALITY

Verify before forecasting

Obtain continuous transaction dates and audited inventory data before treating spikes as seasonality or making stock decisions.

PROMOTIONS

Review PR16 concentration

Investigate the eight PR16 Portable Heater records and compare campaign results with a valid baseline.

ASSORTMENT

Diversify revenue exposure

Monitor Portable Heater concentration alongside margin, units sold, and exposure across the six observed stores.

!

Important analytical limitation

The 2017 extract contains 18 distinct recorded dates (the first two days of April–December), and only six stores appear in the analyzed data. Currency is not identified. Revenue is not profit; these descriptive results cannot establish causal promotional lift or reliable monthly seasonality. Inventory conclusions require stock records.

THE TAKEAWAY

Analysis is valuable when
it changes the next question.

The strongest finding is revenue concentration by product, promotion, and observed period. The next business step is to validate coverage, economics and promotional attribution before acting.
